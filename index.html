Locking Down Apps at Cribl: RBAC, Elevated Permissions, and User Identity | Cribl

Cribl’s June release introduces enhanced security and functionality for Cribl Apps.

  • Role-Based Access Control (RBAC) for Apps is now available, allowing administrators to share installed Apps with specific Members or Teams, limiting access based on user roles.
  • Declarative permission elevation allows App authors to request elevated API access needed by the App, reviewed and approved by administrators during installation and upgrades.
  • Apps can now directly access the signed-in member's identity (ID, name, and profile details) for personalization and internal authorization.
  • These features enhance app governance, security, and personalization capabilities within the Cribl platform.

From prompt to action: Why agent workflows need observability | Cribl

  • Cribl emphasizes the need to move beyond simply observing AI model prompts and responses to observing the entire "agent workflow" – the sequence of actions an AI takes, including information retrieval, tool calls, and decisions.
  • The article details five layers of observable agent workflows: Request, Context, Tool, Decision, and Action, outlining the specific telemetry needed for each layer to understand why an AI agent took a particular action.
  • Cribl’s telemetry pipeline architecture is positioned as a solution to collect, normalize, govern, and correlate workflow signals across multiple systems, providing a single operational view of AI agent activity.
  • The core claim is that true AI observability requires visibility into the complete path from request to action, enabling trust, explainability, and governance – going beyond isolated event logging to understand the entire workflow execution.

In AdTech, AI Is moving faster than the infrastructure beneath it

Ocient highlights a shift in AdTech where companies are prioritizing AI outcomes over AI workloads themselves.

  • Organizations are moving past AI experimentation and focusing on operationalizing AI for measurable business impact, scalability, and governance.
  • The discussion emphasized a gap between rapid AI adoption and the ability of existing data infrastructure to support production-scale AI deployment (Gartner estimates only 2-3% of organizations have enterprise-grade AI deployment).
  • Agentic AI, where a single request triggers multiple queries, significantly increases infrastructure demands beyond traditional analytics workloads.
  • Strong data governance and compliance (like GDPR) remain critical, even with AI adoption, and infrastructure is increasingly viewed as a key competitive differentiator ("the moat").

Cortex ITDR: Detecting Cyber threats in Google Workspace - Palo Alto Networks Blog

  • Palo Alto Networks Cortex XSIAM now features Identity Threat Detection and Response (ITDR) capabilities for Google Workspace, addressing the increasing shift of attacks targeting the SaaS identity layer.
  • The platform detects attacks leveraging “living off the land” techniques—such as OAuth consent phishing, credential harvesting via the Drive API (using tools like GD-Thief), and abuse of legitimate features—that bypass traditional security controls.
  • Cortex XSIAM establishes behavioral baselines for users and entities within Google Workspace, identifying deviations from normal activity (e.g., unauthorized app access, unusual file access patterns) and correlating multiple signals into high-severity incidents.
  • Specific alerts are mapped to MITRE ATT&CK techniques, covering areas like domain policy modification, credential access, data exfiltration, and impairment of defenses, offering comprehensive threat coverage within Google Workspace.

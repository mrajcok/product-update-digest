Introducing AWS Cloud Connections: Automated AWS log discovery for Cribl Stream | Cribl

Cribl Stream’s new AWS Cloud Connections feature automates the discovery and onboarding of AWS logs.

  • Cloud Connections enumerates log destinations across AWS organizations or accounts and generates CloudFormation templates for streamlined onboarding to Cribl.
  • The feature addresses challenges with logs scattered across multiple AWS services (CloudWatch, S3, Firehose) and regions.
  • It operates by generating a CloudFormation template to create a read-only STS role, enabling access to AWS services for log discovery via APIs.

Introducing SecIT Bench, Cribl’s AI telemetry benchmark: A new standard for evaluating AI models in IT and security workflows | Cribl

Cribl introduced SecIT Bench, a benchmark for evaluating AI agents used in IT and security workflows, testing performance across real-world incident scenarios.

  • SecIT Bench evaluated 14 AI models across 30 incident scenarios – intrusion/breach, data egress, service errors, and performance degradation – measuring accuracy, cost, and runtime.
  • Testing included two “weight classes” to isolate model capabilities from the surrounding environment (“harness”), revealing that a coding harness was 23% less cost-efficient than a lightweight harness.
  • Grok-4.6 achieved the top score (80.7%), but the top four models were statistically tied, and cost varied significantly (ranging from $0.28 to $7.34 per session) with no direct correlation to accuracy.
  • The benchmark highlights that current AI models excel at initial investigation stages, but purpose-built tooling is needed to close the loop end-to-end, focusing on efficient telemetry handling and investigation-specific action grammars.

Ocient blog · 2026-08-13

The infrastructure tax of agentic AI in AdTech

  • Ocient highlights a significant "infrastructure tax" incurred when deploying agentic AI systems in AdTech, stemming from the intensive, iterative querying required by agents – replacing a single human query with 30-50 sequential, dependent queries.
  • The article claims that existing consumption-priced data systems (like Snowflake and BigQuery) are not optimized for this agent-driven query load, as they were designed for a lower, human-paced query cadence, leading to unexpectedly high bills.
  • A parallel challenge arises with outcomes-based measurement, requiring trillion-row joins of panel, purchase, and bid-stream data with low latency—exposing the same underlying infrastructure limitations.
  • Beyond cost, the piece emphasizes the need for “auditability at machine speed” to reconstruct agent decision-making in case of errors or unexpected behavior, positioning this as a foundational infrastructure requirement, not an afterthought.
